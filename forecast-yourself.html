Forecast yourself, then meet the experts

In early 2025, Lucius Caviola and Bradford Saad asked 67 people who work on digital minds, AI, philosophy, and forecasting to put numbers on the future of computer systems that might have subjective experience. You are about to answer the same questions.

For each one, read what the experts said for and against before you commit a number. At the end you will see your forecasts next to theirs. The goal is not to match them. It is to notice where you differ and be able to say why.

Definitions the survey used: a digital mind is any computer system with a capacity for subjective experience, with a welfare capacity at least roughly that of a typical human, created by our own civilization. Some questions ask you to assume the first digital mind is a machine learning system created by 2040, whether or not you find that likely. Considerations below are paraphrased from the report; they are views held by at least one participant, not consensus.

    What to do with this

    Pick the two questions where your number is furthest from the median. For each, go back and reread the consideration on the other side. Then write one sentence: did it move you, and if not, what do you know that the experts did not weigh? That sentence is a week 1 homework artifact. Bring it to the session.
