# About this course

## What it is

Learn about possible AI welfare, assess research, and develop a feasible project proposal with a way to test its value. Projects may involve research, tools, education, practical interventions or governance.

## Who it is for

People considering work on artificial minds. No machine learning or philosophy background is required. Expect substantial reading, writing and peer review.

## What it costs you

Plan for **about 6 hours a week**, or **about 9 if you are new to the field**, plus pre-course preparation. These are planning estimates; actual time will vary. Electives are extra.

## How to read each week

Follow the numbered reading list. Start with the core glossary terms and consult reference entries as needed.

## Terms

Aim to use **at least five of the week's starred glossary terms** correctly in your homework, bolded on first use. Explain how they apply; listing terms is not enough. Homework receives feedback rather than marks.

Weeks 1–7 include a two-minute warm-up defining three terms. Week 8 uses the terms in the final proposal, with no separate warm-up. Unstarred terms are reference material.

## Homework, peer comments, and feedback

**The live session is on day 7 of each week.** The facilitator publishes calendar dates before the course begins. Homework and peer comments are due before the session in every week, so bring your answers and your comment to class.

| Day | Usual schedule: Weeks 1–3, 6 and 7 |
|---|---|
| 4 | Post homework in the course channel |
| 5 | Choose and read a peer's homework |
| 6 | Post your peer comment |
| 7 | Live session |
| 8 | Facilitator posts a cohort note and full feedback for two learners |

Week 4 has two critique deadlines. **Weeks 5 and 8:** post a draft at least **48 hours before the session**, receive feedback in class, and revise by the end-of-week deadline. Live feedback replaces the written peer comment in those weeks.

### Choosing who to comment on

Choose someone who has posted and has no comment. If all posts have comments, choose the person you have reviewed least often. Keep a short record and aim to review at least four different peers across the course, where cohort participation allows.

Use this template, in **150–250 words, about 20 minutes**:

1. **Keep:** quote something that works and explain why.
2. **Change:** suggest a specific revision, including replacement wording.
3. **Question:** ask something whose answer could improve the work.

Week 4 specifies a longer critique. Full facilitator feedback is scheduled at least twice per learner, with the first before Week 6, prioritizing those who have received the least. You may request feedback in a particular week.

## Logistics

- **Cohort:** 5–8 learners.
- **Sessions:** 90 minutes online, including breakouts. Recording requires cohort agreement.
- **Posting:** use the course Slack channel. Head your post `Week N homework: [your name]`, add one line about where you want feedback, and put the work in the thread. Peer comments go in that thread.
- **Absence:** post your homework and read the discussion. Session notes are posted within 48 hours.
- **AI assistance:** you may use it to explore ideas. Homework judgments and writing should be yours; disclose any AI-drafted text.

## The final submission and what happens after

Submit a **one-page project proposal and a 3–5 minute video pitch** by the end of Week 8. Use the [Companion template](02-course-companion.md#project-proposal-template). A draft is due 48 hours before the Week 8 session. You are not required to carry out the project during the course.

Submissions go to the cohort gallery. The following two weeks include a cohort vote, facilitator shortlist and expert review, with seed grants of **$5,000, $2,500 and $2,500**. Every submitted project receives written feedback against the five criteria within three weeks of the deadline.

Projects meeting the course's criteria are recommended to the Project Incubator with financial support; its application is separate. Judges may opt in as mentors or recommend others. Technical projects may be referred to SPAR.

## The five judging criteria

Each is scored 1–5; honest and calibrated claims is the tiebreaker.

1. Honest and calibrated claims
2. Tractability
3. Fills a gap in the field
4. Downside risk, reverse-scored
5. Fit between person and project

Read the [criteria and scoring examples](02-course-companion.md#the-five-judging-criteria-with-anchors-12-min) in Week 1 and revisit them when drafting your proposal. Explain your fit with the project in the video.

## The through-line, week by week

Weeks 1–2 introduce systems, stakes, moral grounds and theories. Weeks 3–4 develop evidence assessment and paper-review skills. Weeks 5–7 examine possible contributions, company actions and governance. Week 8 develops the final proposal.

## Before the live session (1 hr 20)

Draft your one-pager using the [project proposal template](02-course-companion.md#project-proposal-template): **15 min** to review course work and choose a contribution, **55 min** to draft, and **10 min** to check evidence and add a question for reviewers. Build on your Week 5 proposal and subsequent feedback, or explain why you changed projects.

Post the draft **48 hours before the session**. This work is included in the final-submission budget below. Peer feedback happens live; there is no separate peer-comment task.

## Live session (90 min)

1. Apply the judging rubric to draft proposals, **12 min**.
2. Review projects with two peers and record revisions, **62 min**. Each discussion begins with **two minutes** identifying weaknesses before other feedback.
3. Confirm submission instructions and judging timeline; reflect on learning and future contact, **16 min**.

## Final submission, due end of week 8 (3 hr 20 total)

Submit a **one-page project proposal and a 3–5 minute video pitch** to the cohort gallery. You do not have to complete a study or launch an intervention during the course.

**Time budget:** **1 hr 20** for the pre-session draft, **1 hr 30** for revision and checking, and **30 min** to prepare, record and upload the video. The live session is separate.

**One-pager:** use the [seven-section template](02-course-companion.md#project-proposal-template). Aim for **600–750 words**, with short bullets and source links, on one readable page. Include your title, name and feedback question; retain an updated version of that question in the final submission. Remove the template prompts. No separate brainstorm or experiment plan is required.

**Video:** explain the question and target, importance, plan and first test, what would change your mind, and why you are suited to the project. A four-minute outline allocates **30, 45, 90, 45 and 30 seconds** to these topics respectively. Production quality is not judged; fit between person and project is assessed here. The video and one-pager should describe the same project.

# Judging window (the two weeks after week 8)

1. **Days 1–4:** browse the gallery and vote for the top one or two submissions.
2. **Days 5–7:** the facilitator selects the panel shortlist, including the cohort's choices and any additions.
3. **Days 8–14:** the expert panel reviews submissions asynchronously and awards seed grants of **$5,000, $2,500 and $2,500**.
4. **Within three weeks of the submission deadline:** each submitted project receives written feedback against the five criteria, whether shortlisted or not.

**Incubator handoff:** selected projects are recommended to the Project Incubator with financial support; its application is separate. Panel judges may offer mentorship or recommend candidates. Technical projects may be referred to SPAR.
